Where it's from

Your trout's journey

We raise our trout in stages that follow the fish's natural life cycle.

  1. 1. Hatched in spring water

    The eggs hatch in spring water.

  2. 2. Grown in freshwater

    Young trout grow up in our 18 freshwater farms in Denmark.

  3. 3. Finished at sea

    They then move to one of our three sea farms in the Great Belt.

  4. 4. To your store

    From the sea to the pack in your hand.

Nature

How we look after nature

Our fish depend on healthy seas, both where we farm and where their feed comes from.

A first for fish farming

Musholm is the first aquaculture company to create Science Based Targets for Nature, as part of the Science Based Targets Network's Ocean Hub pilot. These are our three targets.

Read the press release →

Less wild fish in our feed

We're cutting the blue whiting used in our feed by more than half, and replacing more wild-caught ingredients with by-products and alternatives.

By 2029

Restoring reefs in the Great Belt

We're helping to restore 42 hectares of rock reef and 14 hectares of blue mussel reef, about 80 football pitches. We're also cutting the anti-fouling treatment on our sea farm nets every year.

By the end of 2029

Protecting sea life where feed is fished

With fishery improvement projects in the North Sea and South America, we're working to protect threatened species from fishing gear.

By 2029
About our ASC certification

Musholm Vest, where most of our trout is raised, is certified to the Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC) standard. It covers the environment, fish welfare and the people who work on the farm.

What it eats

Feed made with nature in mind

Feed is one of the biggest parts of a farmed fish's environmental footprint. Our trout eat Blue Impact feed from BioMar, the Danish feed company, which measures its feed's footprint so it can keep improving it.

Cook it

Ready in 15 minutes

Trout is mild and quick to cook. Here are two easy ways.

Crispy pan-fried trout

10 min · Serves 2
  1. Pat 2 fillets dry and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Heat a little oil in a pan over medium-high heat. Fry skin side down for 4 minutes, until the skin is crisp.
  3. Turn, add a knob of butter, and cook for 1 more minute.
  4. Finish with lemon and fresh dill. Serve with new potatoes.

Oven-baked trout with vegetables

15 min · Serves 2
  1. Heat the oven to 200°C.
  2. Lay 2 fillets on a tray with sliced fennel, cherry tomatoes and a drizzle of olive oil.
  3. Bake for 10–12 minutes, until the fish flakes easily.
Storing: keep in the fridge and eat by the date on the pack.
Is it done? The flesh turns opaque and separates easily with a fork.
