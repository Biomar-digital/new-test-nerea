Out there, we're helping to rebuild 56 hectares of reef

42 hectares of rock reef and 14 hectares of blue mussel reef in the Great Belt, about 80 football pitches, by the end of 2029. We're also cutting the anti-fouling treatment on our sea farm nets every year.

The reefs are one of three targets we have set for nature. The other two are about the wild fish that end up in fish feed.

Less wild fish in our feed

By 2029 we'll cut the blue whiting in our feed by more than half, compared with 2025, and use more by-products and alternative ingredients in place of wild-caught fish.

Safer seas where feed is fished

By 2029, through fishery improvement projects in the North Sea and South America, we're working to reduce harm to threatened species from fishing gear.

Musholm is the first fish farmer to set Science Based Targets for Nature. That means the targets are based on science, set with the Science Based Targets Network, and progress can be checked.

Two ways to cook it tonight

Crispy pan-fried trout

10 minutes · serves 2

  1. Pat 2 fillets dry and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Heat a little oil in a pan over medium-high heat. Fry skin side down for 4 minutes, until the skin is crisp.
  3. Turn, add a knob of butter, and cook for 1 more minute.
  4. Finish with lemon and fresh dill. Serve with new potatoes.

Oven-baked trout with vegetables

15 minutes · serves 2

  1. Heat the oven to 200°C.
  2. Lay 2 fillets on a tray with sliced fennel, cherry tomatoes and a drizzle of olive oil.
  3. Season with salt and pepper, then bake for 10–12 minutes, until the fish flakes easily.
  4. Finish with a squeeze of lemon and fresh parsley. Serve with crusty bread.
