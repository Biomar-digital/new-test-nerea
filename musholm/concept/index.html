Looking after the sea it grows in.

Our trout depend on healthy seas, here in Denmark and where their feed comes from. So we have set three science-based targets for nature.

56ha

Reefs restored in the Great Belt

We're helping to restore 42 hectares of rock reef and 14 hectares of blue mussel reef. That's around 80 football pitches of new habitat for sea life.

By the end of 2029
½

Less wild fish in the feed

We're cutting the blue whiting in our feed by more than half, compared with 2025, and replacing more wild-caught fish with by-products and alternatives.

By 2029
2seas

Protecting sea life where feed is fished

Fishery improvement projects in the North Sea and South America help reduce harm to threatened species from fishing gear.

By 2029

“We want to take responsibility locally, where we raise our fish, but also in the parts of the world where the ingredients for our feed come from.”

Thorbjørn Harkamp, CEO of Musholm

Why “first” matters

Plenty of companies set climate targets. Musholm is the first fish farmer to set targets for nature itself, through the Science Based Targets Network's Ocean Hub pilot. It means our goals for reefs, feed and sea life are based on science, and progress can be checked.

Dinner in fifteen minutes.

Trout is mild, tender and quick to cook. Two easy ways to start.

10 minutes · Serves 2

Crispy pan-fried trout

  1. Pat 2 fillets dry and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Heat a little oil in a pan over medium-high heat. Fry skin side down for 4 minutes, until the skin is crisp.
  3. Turn, add a knob of butter, and cook for 1 more minute.
  4. Finish with lemon and fresh dill. Serve with new potatoes.
15 minutes · Serves 2

Oven-baked trout with vegetables

  1. Heat the oven to 200°C.
  2. Lay 2 fillets on a tray with sliced fennel, cherry tomatoes and a drizzle of olive oil.
  3. Season with salt and pepper, then bake for 10–12 minutes, until the fish flakes easily.
  4. Finish with a squeeze of lemon and fresh parsley. Serve with crusty bread.
